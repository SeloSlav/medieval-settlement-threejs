## Forest ivy lighting diagnosis — 2026-09-05

The user's dark-ivy observation exposed a WebGPU lighting-normal bug. Preserve the existing ivy atlas, tint, leaf geometry, petiole wind, resident stream, shadow policy, and all distance rules. The accepted meadow appearance is outside this correction.

`ivy-material-inspection-02` isolates original, neutral and partially lifted tints under directional and ambient-only illumination. The dark green tint contributes to the appearance, but the full game view shows that brightening it does not explain or fix the nearly black leaves. These tint experiments are review-only; no production tint or texture change was installed. All ten standalone captures had no browser errors, with identical draws and triangles per view.

`ivy-world-inspection-02` frames a real resident leaf at (197.57, -36.77), avoiding placement plans subsequently cleared for roads. All three tint arms submit 129 draws and 21,006,352 triangles, with 23,680 ivy leaves resident. This is structural evidence, not an FPS benchmark. The earlier `ivy-world-inspection-01` framed a road-cleared plan and must not be used to judge ivy quality.

`ivy-world-lighting-inspection-01` separates final, lighting, no-AO and normal diagnostics. Some surrounding darkness comes from the lighting/post treatment, but the ivy's normal buffer points sideways while the terrain and upward leaves should align. Ivy does not directly receive tree shadow maps: the existing `terrain-projected` ground-cover policy has both `receiveShadow` and `castShadow` false. Post ambient occlusion and surrounding terrain shadows still affect the presentation. The no-AO diagnostic also bypasses the optional structure treatment, so its full image difference must not be attributed solely to AO.

`ivy-normal-transport-inspection-01` confirms the cause in the compiled production-pass WGSL. The old vertex program passes the raw prototype normal (`varyings.nodeVarying11 = normal`) into fragment-stage hinge lighting; it loses the instance basis that turns prototype +Z into an upward leaf face. Explicitly interpolating the post-instance, hinged view normal fixes that direction. The fragment now uses `normalView = normalize(vIvyInspectionNormal)` and the direction agrees with the terrain. Original tint and atlas immediately recover readable green leaves and veins. All six captures have no browser errors. The material's interstage varying locations decrease from 11 to 9; its vertex inputs and geometry are unchanged.

The equivalent production correction is in `createIvyLeafHingeWindNodes`: carry the computed normal through `vIvyHingeNormal`, then normalize interpolation. TypeScript, forest-floor coverage contracts, and the isolated production build pass. Rain, winter, summer, four maximum-wind phases, and 12 m/42 m views were captured and inspected; the retained atlas/tint remain readable in rain and the existing snow coverage still follows upward leaf faces.

`ivy-normal-transport-paired-01` has twelve 360-frame arms (1,077 GPU samples per representation per distance). Pooled GPU medians: 12 m 21.2255 → 22.3949 ms; 42 m 28.1487 → 27.6777 ms. Repeated controls varied substantially. A shorter, balanced ABBA run, `ivy-normal-transport-balanced-01`, has 48 60-frame arms (708 samples per representation per distance): 12 m 29.2086 → 29.4789 ms (+0.93%); 42 m 24.1674 → 23.0953 ms (-4.44%). Median paired block changes were +0.26% and -3.65%, but individual blocks remained noisy. Both runs have no browser errors and unchanged draws/triangles at each distance (129/21,006,352 near, 133/20,945,464 farther). The host was observed at 99% GPU usage, 86°C and 1,365 MHz during the first run. This establishes no consistent slowdown in these runs, **not a guaranteed FPS equivalence or a claimed speedup**. Shader work and interpolants decrease; broader performance coverage remains part of the active goal.

## Reported grass zoom pop-in — 2026-09-05

The latest user report is an actual regression: `applySeedThreeGrassSeasonMaterial` supplied a custom `opacityNode` that omitted `materialOpacity`. In Three's compiled path a custom opacity node replaces the normal material-opacity accessor, so `GrassBladeField` continued calculating its gradual zoom opacity while the seasonal grass shader ignored it. The entire resident meadow appeared once the mesh visibility gate opened.

The correction multiplies the seasonal opacity by the live `materialOpacity` accessor. All original 200%–400% zoom thresholds, easing, hysteresis, alpha-to-coverage policy, budgets, grass geometry and full-strength texture/seasonal appearance remain intact. The extra existing alpha factor is deliberately preserved to avoid altering the fully visible cutout while restoring the missing fade. The local lightweight TSL declaration now includes the accessor already exported by installed Three r185.1. TypeScript and close-ground vegetation contracts pass; the production build passes.

`inspectGrassFade.mjs` compares the archived broken material with the same material plus the missing factor. It exercises fixed-camera opacity steps and slow 190%→450%→190% zoom routes through the actual scene renderer, recording images, material/LOD telemetry, shaders and video. Rendered-pixel regression results are pending.
