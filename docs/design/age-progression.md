# Ages and historical progression

**Status:** Master plan — four-era spine locked; implementation remains separately authorized
**Last updated:** 2026-08-28
**Project:** Selo Empire

## Decision

Selo Empire progresses through exactly four visually distinct historical **Ages**. The four names, anchor years, order, and regional focus in this document are locked. New work may refine balance and implementation, but it must not add, remove, merge, reorder, or rename an Age without an explicit replacement decision.

An Age is not completed by filling a generic technology tree or proving an abstract list of supply chains. Its ordinary proof is a meaningful cohort at that Age's capstone residence tier—Tier 4, 8, 12, or 16—whose visible needs remain continuously satisfied. Selected Ages also require one explicit, era-defining **Capstone Project**. A Capstone Project proves mastery of the Age's defining network; it is never a disguised checklist of every optional industry.

For the current Croatian Frontier game, the capstone residence remains the existing **Tier 4 home** and no separate Capstone Project is added retroactively. Supplying and maintaining enough occupied Tier 4 homes is itself proof that the settlement has built as much farming, processing, distribution, trade, infrastructure, and institutional capacity as it needs to advance. Optional industries and unused production routes do not become hidden blockers.

The campaign has exactly four playable Ages:

1. **Croatian Frontier — 1550**;
2. **The Karolina Road — 1726**;
3. **Railway and Industrial Municipality — 1873**;
4. **Electrified Socialist Municipality — 1966**.

Four Ages are enough. Together they form a specifically Gorski Kotar infrastructure ladder: pack trails and noble-estate roads become an engineered imperial road, then a mountain railway and industrial municipality, and finally an electrified, motorized, publicly serviced socialist municipality. Each entry must create a large, legible change in architecture, transport, utilities, public space, residence forms, clothing, props, production, and household expectations.

Intermediate periods still matter, but they are handled through generational jumps, chronicles, inherited conditions, and optional historical scenarios rather than receiving additional progression ladders. The World Wars are **historical ruptures between Ages**, not prosperous production Ages the player is rewarded for reaching. The main progression ends in the mature socialist period; post-1990 history is outside this four-Age campaign rather than a fifth Age.

An entered Age is permanent. Shortages can make households lose welfare and stop progress toward the next Age, but they cannot make the calendar run backward, delete knowledge, demote structures, or revoke an Age already reached.

## Geographic scope

The current sandbox is Gorski Kotar, Croatia, in 1550. The four-Age campaign remains locally grounded in that region rather than becoming a generic history of Croatia, Yugoslavia, or the Balkans. Other localities require their own researched manifests if they ever become playable.

The first Age is called **Croatian Frontier**. Historically, the 1550 setting is early modern and late Renaissance, although medieval institutions and material practices persist. The exact anchor years below are content and art-direction boundaries: they identify the historical transformation each Age owns without claiming that every village changed in a single year.

## Design principles

- **Capstone-tier households are the universal progression gate.** The continuous residence ladder ends each Age at Tier 4, 8, 12, or 16. An Age cannot complete until enough occupied dwellings at its capstone tier have maintained all declared needs for the required window.
- **One capstone may prove the defining network.** Ages II–IV also require one named, visible Capstone Project. Age I deliberately retains the existing residence-only proof.
- **Household needs are the economy test.** Goods and services count only when they actually reach the qualifying residences through the authoritative simulation.
- **No parallel economy exam.** There is no requirement to operate every possible supply family. A chain matters to advancement only when it supplies a declared capstone-tier need or the single declared Capstone Project.
- **A cohort prevents showcase-house exploits.** One mansion surrounded by deprivation cannot advance the settlement.
- **Ages are rare visual transformations.** Each Age must justify itself with a strongly different built environment and residence path. Historical periods that do not warrant that production scope are crossed in a time jump.
- **No bridge-item leakage.** Assign a commodity or production chain to the earliest Age in which it has a clear, mature cultural and economic role. An isolated earlier attestation is not a reason to introduce a next-Age item near the end of the current roster.
- **Advancement is earned, previewed, and player-triggered.** Eligibility produces an announcement and confirmation screen; the game never changes Age without the player's choice.
- **History accumulates.** Roads, parcels, landmarks, workshops, ruins, and selected older production methods remain visible as the settlement changes.
- **Residence Tier and Age are separate.** Tier describes prosperity within a residence family. Age determines which residence forms, upgrades, services, and visual language are available.
- **Housing progresses continuously from Tier 1 to Tier 16.** Confirming an Age transition automatically advances every residence at the completed Age's capstone tier: Tier 4→5, Tier 8→9, or Tier 12→13. The residence changes in place while preserving its household population and history; lower-tier homes remain unchanged and continue upgrading normally.
- **Historical politics are systems, not stereotypes.** Identity, religion, nationalism, class, and state power act through institutions, media, policy, and material conditions—not inherent population traits.

## Player-facing vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Historical Age** | The highest Age entered. It is permanent and never decreases. |
| **Age Standard** | The residence needs, services, infrastructure, and visual language introduced by an Age. |
| **Capstone Residence Tier** | The highest residence tier in an Age: Tier 4, 8, 12, or 16. |
| **Capstone Project** | The one public work, where required, that demonstrates the current Age's defining infrastructure network. |
| **Completion Cohort** | The required group of occupied dwellings at the current Age's capstone tier that must pass together. |
| **Maintenance Window** | The rolling period during which every counted residence must remain at its complete, player-visible standard. |
| **Legacy Building** | An earlier-Age non-residential building that remains as working fallback, adaptive reuse, heritage, or ruin. Residences instead remain on the continuous Tier 1–16 ladder. |
| **Historical Rupture** | A war, dissolution, occupation, or systemic break handled as a transition/scenario rather than an advancement tier. |

Do not call Ages “settlement tiers.” Residence tiers, Approval tiers, and the map's Hamlet/Village/Town labels already have separate meanings.

## Core progression loop

1. Build the current Age's economy and services as needed during ordinary play.
2. Upgrade residences through that Age's four-tier band: 1–4, 5–8, 9–12, or 13–16.
3. Establish the required occupied Completion Cohort.
4. Construct and commission the Age's named Capstone Project when that Age requires one.
5. Keep every declared need of the capstone-tier cohort satisfied through one shared Maintenance Window.
6. Use the residence panel or Town Hall summary to find any failing home, need, route, utility, or Capstone Project connection.
7. When the cohort passes and the required project is operational, receive an **Age Ready** announcement and review the next Age's time jump, new needs, unlocks, and visual changes.
8. Choose **Advance Now** or **Stay in This Age**. Staying dismisses the announcement without losing the earned transition; the player can reopen it later from the top-right **Lord’s Profile**. Confirmation crosses the generational time jump and does not start another hidden resource-delivery exam.
9. Begin the new Age with every former capstone-tier residence automatically converted to Tier 5, 9, or 13, while lower-tier homes remain occupied and usable. The next-Age building options unlock at the same moment.

Reaching the final Age opens its endgame and leaves the sandbox running.

## Residence completion contract

### The household advancement proof

The household half of Age completion is derived from the same authoritative residence state the player already manages. If a recurring requirement does not appear as a need, shared-building requirement, or service on the relevant Tier 4, 8, 12, or 16 dwelling, it cannot silently block advancement. The only additional proof permitted is the Age's named Capstone Project.

A qualifying residence must:

- be at the current Age's capstone tier: 4, 8, 12, or 16;
- be occupied rather than an empty shell;
- meet a minimum meaningful occupancy so nearly empty prestige buildings cannot carry the gate;
- have every declared food, water, fuel, goods, utility, institution, access, and comfort need satisfied;
- receive those goods and services through the real road, distribution, utility, or service network;
- remain free of a sustained household warning throughout its Maintenance Window.

The residence need list is the contract. It may contain substitutable goods, but the player needs only one valid path for each substitutable need. Optional orchards, exports, recipes, workshops, military systems, or institutions do not block an Age unless the chosen scenario makes their output an explicit residence need visible before the window begins.

Transient cart timing or a one-tick service refresh should use the household system's normal grace behavior. A sustained failure stops that residence's qualifying timer. The UI must show which home failed, which need failed, and how much maintained time was lost; it must not expose an opaque civilization progress percentage.

### Age I completion cohort

For **Age I — Croatian Frontier**, the capstone is the existing **Tier 4 home**.

The provisional cohort target remains scaled and capped:

`required Tier 4 homes = min(24, max(world-size floor, ceil(20% × peak occupied homes during the Maintenance Window)))`

Suggested starting floors are 8 homes on a Small world, 12 on a Medium world, and 16 on a Large world. These are balance targets, not final constants. Using the trailing peak prevents demolition or short-term depopulation from lowering the target during proof.

Every counted Age I home must:

- average at least the current Tier 1 household baseline of three residents across the window;
- be at structural Tier 4;
- maintain every need shown for a Tier 4 household, including its complete diet, cured provisions, beverage, cloth, shoes, pottery, luxury, water, fuel, faith/civic service, storage, and marketplace access where those remain part of the authoritative Tier 4 contract.

Maintaining that cohort demonstrates all supply capacity actually necessary for Age I completion. There are no additional permanent chain badges and no second checklist for unused industries.

### Continuous Tier 1–16 residence ladder

Residence tier is permanent and monotonically increasing:

| Age | Residence tiers | Capstone tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I — Croatian Frontier | 1–4 | 4 |
| II — The Karolina Road | 5–8 | 8 |
| III — Railway and Industrial Municipality | 9–12 | 12 |
| IV — Electrified Socialist Municipality | 13–16 | 16 |

All Ages reuse the same household rule:

`build the current-Age capstone-tier cohort + maintain all declared needs = household proof passed`

Confirming an Age transition performs one automatic residence rollover. Every Tier 4 residence becomes Tier 5, every Tier 8 residence becomes Tier 9, and every Tier 12 residence becomes Tier 13 at the corresponding boundary. This applies to occupied and empty residences, although an empty upgraded dwelling remains empty and earns no progression credit. The tier, Age-appropriate form, capacity, and need contract change atomically on the existing plot or amalgamated footprint; household identities, family/community records, inventories, and continuous occupancy survive. Residences below the completed capstone tier do not receive a free jump and continue along the ordinary upgrade ladder.

The first tier of a new Age is intentionally a gentle step rather than a second capstone. Its household contract inherits every obligation of the previous tier and normally adds only **one clearly previewed commodity or household product**. A single era-defining network service may take that place when a product would be artificial. The added need appears immediately when the transition completes and uses the ordinary logistics grace period, so the player sees the new requirement at once without suffering an unannounced instant collapse.

| Age boundary | Automatic residence rollover | One immediate added requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Croatian Frontier → The Karolina Road | Tier 4→5 | **Copperware** as a durable household-product need |
| The Karolina Road → Railway and Industrial Municipality | Tier 8→9 | **Lamp oil** for household lighting |
| Railway and Industrial Municipality → Electrified Socialist Municipality | Tier 12→13 | **Electricity connection**, the justified one-service exception |

Any proposal to add more than this single boundary requirement must be explicitly justified, previewed before confirmation, and approved as part of that Age's locked residence contract. Later tiers within the Age introduce the rest of its new goods and services gradually through ordinary player-directed upgrades.

There is no separate “legacy housing” class. An unupgraded house is simply a lower-tier occupied residence. Once upgraded, its former mesh, capacity, and needs cease to exist on that site. Non-residential buildings may still remain legacy, adaptive-reuse, or heritage structures.

Dense buildings count their actually occupied, fully serviced dwelling units, not their exterior shell. The exact cohort size and Maintenance Window may vary with dwelling capacity and game pace, but the underlying rule must not change between Ages.

For Ages II–IV, cohort scaling uses occupied **dwelling units**, not building count:

`required capstone dwellings = min(age cap, max(world-size floor, ceil(age share × peak occupied dwellings during the Maintenance Window)))`

Each unit counts only when it meets the same minimum-occupancy and household-need rules as a detached home and every shared requirement of its building is operational. Capacity by itself never creates residents or progression credit. A large block may contribute several qualifying units only because it also contains, supplies, and services that many real households.

### Capstone Project contract

The final rule is:

`Age complete = household proof passed AND required Capstone Project operational`

Only one project may be mandatory in an Age:

| Age | Required Capstone Project |
| --- | --- |
| I — Croatian Frontier | None; retain the implemented residence-only proof. |
| II — The Karolina Road | **Karolina Trunk-Road Section and Imperial Post Station**. |
| III — Railway and Industrial Municipality | **Mainline Railway Station and Freight Yard**. |
| IV — Electrified Socialist Municipality | **Integrated Municipal Utilities Works**; completing it and the Tier 16 cohort records the final campaign outcome. |

A Capstone Project:

- is announced from the start of its Age and visible in the Age panel;
- uses a small number of legible construction stages such as survey, civil works, superstructure/equipment, and commissioning;
- consumes physical materials through ordinary storage and transport;
- must connect to the relevant road, rail, power, water, or sewer network and be operational at the moment of Age completion;
- may fail temporarily through damage, missing staff, missing fuel, or a broken network, but never deletes completed construction stages;
- does not require the player to run every Age-owned optional industry;
- does not impose a second 120-day timer unless its service is itself a visible capstone-tier household need.

Capstone construction and the household Maintenance Window may proceed concurrently. This prevents a completed settlement from waiting through two consecutive proofs.

### Maintenance Window

Use one concurrent rolling window for the whole cohort. A provisional Age I target is **120 in-game days**, subject to playtesting. The purpose is only to prove sustained household operation rather than a one-day stockpile spike.

Seasonal requirements remain ordinary residence needs. The system must not add a hidden forecast, reserve-horizon score, or separate winter exam. If testing shows 120 days is too long or too easy to schedule around, tune the visible duration or the residence's seasonal needs rather than restoring a second progression system.

## Soft Age transition

Eligibility does not immediately change the world. It raises a prominent but non-blocking **Age Ready** announcement with two clear actions:

- **Advance Now** opens the final transition confirmation.
- **Stay in This Age** closes the announcement and leaves the settlement unchanged.

Choosing to stay permanently records that the next Age has been earned. The option cannot be revoked by a later shortage, damaged project, population change, or expired Maintenance Window; the settlement has already completed the proof. The player may therefore continue building and enjoying the current Age for as long as desired.

While an earned transition remains unused, the top-right **Lord’s Profile** displays an **Age Ready** badge. Its **Historical Age** section shows the completed proof, previews the next Age, and provides an **Enter the Next Age** button. Pressing that button always opens a confirmation dialog; it never advances from a single accidental click.

The confirmation screen previews:

- the next Age and the approximate historical time skipped;
- which capstone-tier residences will automatically become Tier 5, 9, or 13, including their new form and capacity;
- the single new commodity, household product, or declared era-defining service their need contract will require immediately;
- new buildings, utilities, transport, policies, and institutions;
- which existing building visuals will update, remain historic, or gain a retrofit option;
- how population records, stock, treasury, carts, and unfinished work are treated across the time jump;
- any locality-specific historical rupture or campaign choice crossed during the jump.

The confirmation dialog repeats the destination Age, approximate time jump, automatic capstone-tier residence conversion, immediate gentle need increase, major new systems, and any irreversible presentation changes. **Confirm Transition** plays a short chronicle, announcement, or map montage and then enters the new Age; **Cancel** returns to the current Age with readiness preserved. Any required Capstone Project has already been built and commissioned before eligibility, so confirmation never reveals a second project or resource checklist.

Immediately after the new Age loads, show a brief **New Age narrator card** in the same style as the opening card that introduces the Habsburg, Venetian, and other regional context. It contains only two compact notes:

- **The new narrative:** no more than two short sentences explaining the changed historical situation and the settlement's new role.
- **Where to begin:** no more than three optional icon callouts pairing a key new resource with a useful early building or network. These are suggestions, not objectives or hidden requirements.

The player can dismiss the card immediately and reread it later in the Chronicle.

Age entry should feel soft in simulation terms and strong in presentation:

- every capstone-tier residence automatically advances to the first tier of the new Age, and the remaining residence upgrades and Age-owned build cards unlock;
- a declared set of continuing buildings can swap to new Age-appropriate meshes, materials, props, or frontage treatments where the change is purely visual and preserves function and footprint;
- the automatic capstone-tier rollover may apply its declared next-tier residence form, capacity, and materials in place; lower-tier residences never receive an automatic reskin;
- functional improvements such as electricity, sewerage, heating, rail access, or modern logistics require explicit player-built retrofits;
- important older buildings and coherent historic districts remain as legacy architecture instead of being universally reskinned;
- the first new-Age residence need appears immediately on automatically rolled-over homes but is limited to the one declared boundary requirement and receives the visible ordinary logistics grace period;
- later needs apply only when the player performs further residence upgrades within the Age.

The Age manifest must list every automatic mesh replacement. A visual swap may not change capacity, inventory, staffing, service area, or upkeep behind the player's back.

## Residences across Ages

Housing is the physical through-line of the campaign. A residence advances from Tier 1 toward Tier 16 rather than restarting or becoming a detached legacy population problem at every Age boundary.

- A transition automatically converts all Tier 4→5, Tier 8→9, or Tier 12→13 residences. Homes below the completed capstone tier keep their current tier, residents, inventory, and needs until upgraded normally.
- An automatic rollover or ordinary single-plot upgrade replaces the old residence tier, mesh, capacity, and need contract in place. It does not create a second building, duplicate residents, or leave a defunct shell behind.
- The upgrade preview states construction cost, capacity change, new needs, utility requirements, backyard/outbuilding effects, and any temporary construction disruption.
- Household records, tenure/history, health, skills, and ordinary possessions survive the upgrade. Goods that no longer fit move through a declared storage or salvage rule rather than disappearing.
- The one declared boundary need activates immediately when the automatic rollover completes: copperware at Tier 5, lamp oil at Tier 9, or electricity at Tier 13. Needs from later tiers activate only after their ordinary physical upgrade completes. Both use the standard visible logistics grace period.
- Lower-tier homes may remain occupied indefinitely, but only Tier 4, 8, 12, or 16 dwellings count toward completion of their corresponding Age.

### Multi-plot residential amalgamation

Age III introduces limited amalgamation for row houses and courtyard housing. Age IV expands it to Yugoslav modernist housing: low- and mid-rise walk-up blocks, long apartment slabs, point blocks or residential towers, and larger mixed residential buildings. The neutral period terms **socialist apartment block**, **modernist apartment slab**, and **point-block tower** should be used in design and player-facing text.

The player may select two or more adjacent, road-connected residence plots and create one redevelopment site:

1. The preview shows the combined footprint, target residence tier, dwelling-unit capacity, shared-service load, displaced gardens/outbuildings, construction stages, and temporary housing plan.
2. Plot merger never lowers tier. A same-tier density conversion retains its tier; an era upgrade raises the finished building to the explicitly purchased target tier.
3. Combining lower-tier plots never grants free tier advancement. Every input dwelling must satisfy the target's upgrade prerequisites and its share of construction cost.
4. Construction is staged so households are not deleted or silently made homeless: the first habitable section is built, households transfer into completed units, then remaining old structures are cleared and later sections finish. If the chosen plan cannot preserve occupied capacity, the preview requires explicit temporary housing before work begins.
5. Household identities and occupants transfer into specific new dwelling units. Surplus units begin empty and fill only through ordinary population growth or migration.
6. Private gardens, pens, orchards, sheds, and stored goods affected by the merger are listed explicitly. They are transferred, salvaged, converted into communal space, or removed according to the confirmed plan.
7. The resulting building has one structural tier but many dwelling units. Each occupied unit has its own food, goods, welfare, and qualification state.
8. Shared stairs, roof, lifts where appropriate, heating, water pressure, sewerage, electricity, fire safety, refuse, and access are building-level requirements. A shared failure affects every dependent unit.
9. Higher density creates proportionally higher consumption, peak utility load, waste, fire/evacuation pressure, traffic, transit demand, and public-service demand. Density is spatial efficiency, not cheaper progression.
10. The amalgamated structure replaces its input houses when their households have safely transferred; those former residences do not remain as simulated shells or duplicate population ledgers.

For progression, a dense building contributes only the occupied units that individually meet the current capstone-tier contract. One empty Tier 16 apartment shell therefore contributes zero; a fully occupied and fully supplied block may legitimately contribute many because its material and service burden scales with those households.

## Continuity of the physical settlement

An Age transition is a generational jump, not a new map.

Every non-residential building and infrastructure asset belongs to one of four categories. Residences instead follow the continuous Tier 1–16 replacement and amalgamation rules above.

1. **Visual continuation** — keeps its function and footprint but may receive an automatic Age-appropriate mesh/material variant.
2. **Retrofittable** — can gain a new utility, safety, power, frontage, or logistics connection through player action.
3. **Adaptively reusable** — can become a school, workshop, cooperative store, clinic, cultural site, or other later use.
4. **Legacy/obsolete** — remains as low-throughput fallback, heritage, ruin, or a candidate for deliberate demolition.

Earlier infrastructure must not be dead content. Historic roads can shape later streets, rail approaches, and utility corridors. Traditional production can provide low-throughput resilience. Preserved buildings and districts can later create civic, cultural, educational, or visitor value.

The day/month simulation cannot run for four literal centuries. Transitions advance macro-history by decades or generations while ordinary seasonal simulation continues inside each Age. Household identities may become family lines or community records so continuity survives even though individuals do not live across the whole campaign.

Before confirmation, the transition preview must state exactly how perishables, durable stock, active carts, unfinished projects, population records, and treasury balances survive or are normalized. A montage must not silently duplicate, destroy, or preserve impossible physical state.

## Four-Age historical ladder

This is the final spine for the Gorski Kotar campaign. It is a visual, infrastructure, and production ladder, not a claim that the years between its anchors were historically empty.

### Age I — Croatian Frontier

**Anchor:** 1550.

**Historical identity:** a cold late-Renaissance Croatian mountain frontier shaped by Frankopan and Zrinski estates, nearby Habsburg–Ottoman conflict, Adriatic trade, forest subsistence, and older local institutions.

**Visual identity:** timber and masonry houses, shingle and tile roofs, household plots, pack trails and handcart roads, mills, craft yards, parish and estate landmarks, frontier works, and a small Adriatic-facing rural market network.

**Economic identity:** household farming, forest extraction, pack-animal trade, hand production, preservation, ale, apple and pear cider, mead, and wine. Strong distilled spirits do not enter this Age's commodity roster merely because isolated sixteenth-century attestations exist.

**Capstone:** a maintained cohort of occupied Tier 4 Croatian Frontier homes.

Once that cohort qualifies, further Age I work should prioritize polish, balance, resilience, historical texture, and readability rather than absorbing industries whose mature identity belongs to Age II.

### Generational jump — from estate frontier to imperial corridor

The chronicle crosses the consolidation of the Military Frontier, the Zrinski and Frankopan period and its end, the wars of the seventeenth century, the 1699 settlement, Habsburg/Cameral control, migration, and the shift from threatened mountain estates toward an imperial route between the interior and the Adriatic. These changes shape inherited ownership, settlement records, landmarks, obligations, and road alignments without becoming a separate Age.

### Age II — The Karolina Road

**Anchor:** 1726, when construction of the Karolina road began through Gorski Kotar.

**Historical identity:** an early-eighteenth-century Habsburg road frontier in which mercantilist policy and an engineered Karlovac–Adriatic connection begin reorganizing settlement and trade.

**Visual identity:** surveyed road alignments, cut slopes, gravel and stone roadbeds, retaining walls, drainage, stone bridges, wagon yards, post stages, tollhouses, customs points, roadside inns, larger stables, sawmills, charcoal yards, and Baroque religious and administrative buildings.

**Economic identity:** draft-animal and wagon logistics, road construction and maintenance, timber and sawn-lumber exports, charcoal, ironworking, grain and salt transit, imperial contracts, hospitality, and higher-value imported goods.

**Signature commodity — rakija:** rakija belongs wholly to Age II, not as a late Age I bridge item. A distillery converts fermented fruit, cider, wine, or suitable by-products with firewood and copper equipment into a compact, durable, high-value spirit. It supports trade, hospitality, luxury, and remedies; it is not another interchangeable everyday thirst drink.

**New pressures:** road gradient, bridge and drainage upkeep, winter closure, wagon and draft-animal capacity, convoy safety, banditry and smuggling, toll policy, fire risk, and dependence on distant markets.

**Capstone direction:** a maintained cohort of prosperous roadside households, coachmasters, merchants, or upgraded Baroque homes supplied with dependable road access, fuel, varied food, household wares, hospitality goods, and the Age's declared civic and religious services.

### Generational jump — reform, the Lujzijana, and municipal modernization

The 1777–78 administrative formation of Gorski Kotar, the Lujzijana road begun in 1803, Napoleonic and Habsburg reforms, the end of feudal obligations, literacy, schooling, cadastral administration, migration, and national movements appear in the chronicle and inherited map. The Lujzijana is an important engineered upgrade and route choice, but it is not a fifth Age or a near-duplicate road ladder.

### Age III — Railway and Industrial Municipality

**Anchor:** 1873, when the Karlovac–Rijeka railway opened through Gorski Kotar.

**Historical identity:** a steam-rail mountain corridor tying timber-producing settlements and municipal centers to Rijeka, Zagreb, Budapest, and wider export markets.

**Visual identity:** cuttings, embankments, tunnels, rails, stations, water towers, signals, depots, warehouses, steam locomotives, industrial sawmills, denser plastered streets, civic schools and offices, worker housing, street lighting, and the beginnings of piped municipal services.

**Economic identity:** timetable- and capacity-bound rail logistics, coal and water supply, machine maintenance, bulk timber and manufactured-wood export, industrial employment, municipal finance, print and education, larger shops, and early tourism.

**New pressures:** rail bottlenecks, locomotive servicing, industrial fire, sanitation, worker housing, food supply for denser settlements, pollution, labor relations, market shocks, and uneven access between station towns and bypassed villages.

**Capstone direction:** a maintained cohort of industrial-era masonry homes, townhouses, or worker dwellings with reliable food and fuel distribution, sanitation, education, municipal services, consumer wares, and access to the road-and-rail network.

### Generational jump — wars, reconstruction, and socialist development

The First World War, interwar state, economic crisis, Second World War, occupation, resistance, civilian loss, postwar reprisals, reconstruction, nationalization, and early socialist reforms are historical ruptures and transition history rather than economic Ages. Any playable wartime scenario requires precise local research and must focus on civilian survival, disrupted networks, relief, and remembrance rather than rewarding violence.

### Age IV — Electrified Socialist Municipality

**Anchor:** 1966, representing the mature modernization drive and the 1966–76 investment period in which much of the region's tourism infrastructure expanded.

**Historical identity:** a self-managed Yugoslav municipality balancing public services, wood industry, transport, tourism, consumer modernization, and migration between mountain settlements and larger cities.

**Visual identity:** paved motor roads, buses, trucks and private cars, power lines and substations, piped water and sewerage, district and household heating, mechanized forestry, wood-processing complexes, schools, clinics, municipal offices, social housing, apartment buildings, hotels, mountain lodges, reservoirs, recreational grounds, and civic monuments.

**Economic identity:** electricity, fuel and motor logistics, socially owned enterprises, mechanized forestry, higher-throughput wood products, construction materials, public-service staffing, domestic and international tourism, retail consumer goods, guest-worker remittances, and municipal investment.

**New pressures:** grid and utility maintenance, snow clearance, motor-road safety, housing allocation, enterprise productivity, public-service coverage, pollution and forest stewardship, investment tradeoffs, regional inequality, out-migration, labor shortages, debt, and inflation.

**Capstone direction:** a maintained cohort of fully serviced detached homes and/or apartment dwellings with electricity, safe water, sanitation, heating, communications, consumer supply, transit access, education, health care, and a functioning local employment base. Industrial output alone cannot complete the final Age.

Age IV is the endgame. It may contain internal early, mature, and late-socialist chapters, but there is no fifth contemporary Age. Completing its capstone records an outcome for the community and leaves the sandbox running.

## Locked Age content manifests

These manifests are the master ownership list for future design. The four Ages and their defining networks are locked. Individual code identifiers, quantities, recipes, staffing, capacities, and balance values remain implementation decisions, but a resource, building, or system may not drift into an earlier Age merely to fill its menu.

### Cross-Age content rules

- **Introduction ownership is permanent.** A good first introduced in Age II remains an Age II good even when later Ages still produce or consume it. Rakija therefore begins in Age II and never appears as a late Age I unlock.
- **Persistence is not reintroduction.** Food, timber, stone, older roads, and traditional workshops can continue into later Ages as legacy production. Later manifests list only genuinely new tracked content or a materially new industrial form.
- **Prefer meaningful logistics.** Track a material only when storage, hauling, scarcity, substitution, or network capacity creates a useful decision. Steam, lodging, schooling, health care, mail, and electricity are services or flows unless stockpiling them is physically meaningful.
- **Upgrades do not counterfeit new resources.** A steam sawmill may process the same logs as a water sawmill at a different rate. It does not need a renamed “industrial log” solely to announce a new Age.
- **Construction materials remain physical.** Road aggregate, lime, rails, machine parts, concrete, pipes, and electrical equipment are delivered to their worksites rather than paid away as abstract progress points.
- **Every tracked good has a declared sink.** A proposed resource must feed a household need, construction, upkeep, an institution, transport, or trade. Decorative inventory is not sufficient justification.

- **Freight wagon and stagecoach:** higher capacity/speed than Age I carts but dependent on grades, wheel condition, fodder, and road maintenance.

- Toll and customs policy, post and passenger schedules, imperial construction contracts, smuggling, banditry, and convoy escort.
- Distillation with recipe selection, fire risk, copper-equipment condition, high-value trade, and restrained household demand.
- Road-induced settlement growth: frontage desirability, inns, warehouses, workshops, and congestion compete for trunk-road access.

#### Capstone Project — Karolina Trunk-Road Section and Imperial Post Station

The player commissions one continuous engineered road between the two designated regional gates and completes its post-station compound. The staged project includes survey, drainage/civil works, bridges or culverts required by the chosen alignment, road surfacing, milestones, and station commissioning. It is operational when the route is continuous, passable to a loaded freight wagon, staffed at the post station, and connected to external trade. Arbitrary kilometers, ownership of every Age II industry, or a specific rakija recipe are not additional gates.

## Historical and ethical guardrails

- Scope the main chronology to Gorski Kotar and its actual connections with Croatian, Habsburg, Hungarian, Adriatic, and Yugoslav institutions; do not use “the Balkans” as if it were one state or one people.
- Avoid “ancient ethnic hatreds,” “tribal,” “backward Balkans,” and similar deterministic explanations.
- Do not project modern national borders or fixed identities backward into Age I or Age II.
- Represent Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, Roma, Muslim, Slovene, Czech, Slovak, German, Hungarian, Vlach-designated, and other lives where locally relevant, using period- and locality-specific terminology and recognizing that identities and official categories changed over time.
- Political and administrative change acts through law, property, taxation, labor, education, military obligation, media, and institutions—not inherent population bonuses.
- Do not make ethnic homogeneity, forced assimilation, territorial expansion, propaganda, expulsion, or atrocity an optimal strategy.
- Multiperspectivity means representing different civilian experiences and political viewpoints. It does not mean presenting court-established crimes as unknowable “competing narratives.”
- Treat both World Wars through civilian consequences, precise local history, and remembrance rather than spectacle. Commission regional historians and sensitivity readers before locking any playable 1914–18 or 1941–45 scenario.
- Post-1990 history is outside the locked four-Age progression. Any future treatment requires a separate scope decision and cannot be smuggled into Age IV's endgame.
- Preserve an explicit distinction between the historically bounded campaign and any counterfactual sandbox path.

## Avoiding grind and exploits

- Count sustained, occupied dwellings at the current capstone tier—4, 8, 12, or 16—rather than production totals or building shells.
- Use the residence's real need satisfaction; do not duplicate those rules in a hidden Age ledger.
- Scale and cap the cohort so one showcase home cannot pass and large worlds do not become punitive.
- Apply meaningful minimum occupancy and count serviced dwelling units inside dense housing.
- Count only goods and services that actually reach the relevant residence.
- Let any valid local or trade-supported route satisfy a substitutable residence need.
- Keep the Maintenance Window visible and use ordinary household warning grace.
- Freeze an Age's capstone need contract for established saves or explicitly grandfather new requirements.
- Never require an Age-entry checklist again after a later shortage.
- Keep each mandatory Capstone Project singular, named, staged, and visible from Age entry.
- Allow project construction and household qualification to overlap; never force two sequential maintenance grinds.
- Before **Age Ready** is earned, a damaged project can delay completion while it is inoperative, but cannot erase the household cohort's already maintained time unless the damage also causes a visible household need failure. After **Age Ready** is earned, its transition entitlement is latched and cannot be revoked.

2. Does it satisfy a declared residence need, establish an Age-defining network, or deepen a central historical tension?
3. Is it required for the current capstone-tier residence, the single Capstone Project, an elective strategy, or visual texture?

## Acceptance criteria for the eventual system

- The campaign contains exactly the four locked Ages named and dated in this document, each with a clearly different world silhouette, network, and residence path.
- Residence progression is one continuous Tier 1–16 ladder, divided into the locked bands 1–4, 5–8, 9–12, and 13–16.

- Residences below the completed capstone tier remain unchanged and receive no free tier advancement.
- The first-tier need increase at each Age boundary activates immediately and is previewed before confirmation: copperware at Tier 5, lamp oil at Tier 9, and electricity at Tier 13. No other boundary need is added without an explicit contract change.
- Two or more adjacent, road-connected residence plots can be amalgamated only through a confirmed redevelopment plan that lists every affected household, asset, capacity change, construction stage, and temporary-housing requirement.
- Amalgamation never lowers a residence tier or grants a free tier increase, and it never deletes, duplicates, or silently evicts a household.
- Dense residential buildings count occupied dwelling units rather than building shells; each counted unit must meet its household contract while all shared-building services remain operational.
- Increased residential capacity does not create population. Spare units fill only through ordinary household formation or migration and contribute no progression credit while empty.
- The current Age completes only through a maintained cohort at Tier 4, 8, 12, or 16 plus its one named Capstone Project where required.
- Croatian Frontier completion remains based on occupied Tier 4 homes and their actual authoritative needs, with no retroactive project.
- The Karolina Road cannot complete without an operational engineered trunk-road section and post station.
- Railway and Industrial Municipality cannot complete without an operational mainline station and freight yard.
- Electrified Socialist Municipality cannot record its final outcome without an operational integrated utilities works serving its Tier 16 cohort.
- No unused industry, unnamed project, or separate supply-family badge can block advancement.
- Empty, nearly empty, disconnected, or briefly supplied residence shells cannot carry the cohort.
- Every failure is explained through a specific residence need, route, utility, staffing issue, or declared project stage the player already understands.
- Qualification produces an announcement and player-controlled confirmation, not an automatic transition.
- The Age Ready announcement offers **Advance Now** and **Stay in This Age**; choosing to stay preserves the earned transition indefinitely without changing the settlement.
- An unused earned transition remains visible in the top-right Lord’s Profile and can be initiated there later only through an explicit confirmation dialog.
- Later shortages, damage, or cohort changes do not revoke an already earned transition entitlement.
- An accepted transition shows one dismissible New Age narrator card with at most two short narrative sentences and three optional resource/building starting suggestions, and the card remains available in the Chronicle.
- Confirmation performs a soft time jump, automatically rolls capstone residences into the next tier, activates their gentle new need, and unlocks the remaining residence upgrades and Age-owned build options.
- Declared visual-only mesh replacements preserve building state and gameplay values exactly.
- Earlier buildings and roads remain useful and visually present unless the player replaces them.
- Advancing never surprises the player with immediate universal household failure: the single rollover requirement is shown before confirmation and uses the ordinary visible logistics grace period.
- Historical rupture content cannot be treated as an economic Age or victory ladder.
- The first new Age feels like a transformation of the same settlement, not a new game loaded on top of it.

## Non-goals

- A fifth playable Age or any split that changes the four locked anchors without an explicit replacement decision.
- A generic Civilization-style technology tree detached from household life.
- A parallel Chain Mastery checklist in addition to residence needs and the single declared Capstone Project.
- Literal simulation of every day from 1550 to 1966.
- Automatic Age advancement at a calendar date or the instant eligibility is reached.
- Forcing the player to advance immediately or permanently losing the option when the Age Ready announcement is dismissed.
- Resetting residence tiers at an Age boundary or creating a parallel legacy-housing population ledger.
- Automatically replacing every old building with a modern mesh.
- Deleting or auto-demolishing obsolete buildings during transition.
- Merging occupied plots in a way that silently destroys households, inventories, gardens, or outbuildings.
- Letting an empty apartment block, nominal capacity, or one serviced lobby stand in for qualified households.
- Revoking historical knowledge after an ordinary shortage.
- Making every commodity variant mandatory.
- Forcing this Gorski Kotar chronology onto another Croatian or regional locality without its own researched manifest.
- Turning war, nationalism, ethnic homogeneity, or displacement into a score-maximizing path.

- What are the final map-size cohort floors, cap, occupancy rule, and Maintenance Window?
- What exact capacities and numerical needs define the locked residence forms in Ages II–IV?
- Which proposed resource identifiers should remain discrete and which should be represented as building condition or network capacity during prototyping?
- What are the final construction quantities, staffing, commissioning tests, and failure rules for the three named Capstone Projects?
- Which existing meshes receive an automatic visual continuation at each transition, and which require a retrofit or remain historic?
- Is the player a continuous settlement institution, a family line, a municipality, or an abstract civic stewardship across generations?
- Which Gorski Kotar subregions and route gates are available in the main campaign, and which require locality-specific scenario manifests?
- Are the World Wars chronicle transitions, separately reviewed survival scenarios, or a mixture by campaign mode?
- What exact plot counts, footprints, unit capacities, construction stages, and temporary-housing options define each Age III and Age IV amalgamation form?
- Which final Age IV outcomes express resilient utilities, viable employment, public services, housing, and environmental stewardship without turning one political program into a universal score?

## Historical research baseline

These are starting points for terminology and chronology, not substitutes for commissioned regional review:

- Robert Skenderović, [“The Population of Gorski kotar in the Early Modern Period and the 19th Century: From Pre-Modern Society to the First Changes Caused by Modernisation”](https://doi.org/10.21857/ypn4oc4l59).
- Hrvatska enciklopedija, [“Vojna krajina”](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/vojna-krajina) — sixteenth-century frontier consolidation, Karlovac, and post-1699 changes.
- Hrvatska enciklopedija, [“Čabar”](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/cabar) — Zrinski iron smelting, seventeenth-century migration, fire, and Cameral ownership.
- Gorski Kotar regional history, [“Gorski kotar”](https://gorskikotar.eu/gorski-kotar/) — Karolina construction and its effect on regional transport and settlement.
- Hrvatska tehnička enciklopedija, [“Ceste”](https://tehnika.lzmk.hr/ceste/) — Karolina as the first hard-surfaced road across the Croatian mountain barrier.
- Jelena Mrgić, [“Aqua vitae — Notes on Geographies of Alcohol Production and Consumption in the Ottoman Balkans”](https://doi.org/10.21301/eap.v12i4.14) — sixteenth- to eighteenth-century distillation context and the later spread of household production.
- Hrvatska tehnička enciklopedija, [“Projektiranje željeznica”](https://tehnika.lzmk.hr/projektiranje-zeljeznica/) — construction and 1873 opening of the Karlovac–Rijeka line and its timber-export function.
- Hrvatska tehnička enciklopedija, [“DIP Delnice”](https://tehnika.lzmk.hr/dip-delnice-delnice/) — regional sawmilling and the transition from water and steam power to postwar wood-industry combines.
- Hrvatska tehnička enciklopedija, [“Vodoopskrba”](https://tehnika.lzmk.hr/vodoopskrba/) — 1959–75 expansion of water networks through Delnice and surrounding settlements.
- Hrvatska tehnička enciklopedija, [“Hidroenergetski sustavi”](https://tehnika.lzmk.hr/hidroenergetski-sustavi/) — the Vinodol hydro system, reservoirs, and their energy, water, recreation, and tourism effects.
- Branko Blažević, [“Razvoj turizma u Hrvatskom Primorju i Gorskom kotaru 1945.–1980.”](https://hrcak.srce.hr/331348) — especially the 1966–76 tourism-infrastructure investment period.
- United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, [“Yugoslavia”](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/yugoslavia).
- Glenn E. Curtis, ed., [_Yugoslavia: A Country Study_](https://www.loc.gov/item/91040323/) — background for the final Age; its research ended in December 1990.
- 1914–1918 Online, [“Yugoslavia”](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/yugoslavia/).
- Barbara Jelavich, [_History of the Balkans_](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/history-of-the-balkans/E478B46D1EB3F80948EF66B1A777F5CC).
- John R. Lampe, [_Yugoslavia as History: Twice There Was a Country_](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/73577/frontmatter/9780521773577_frontmatter.pdf).
- Saul Estrin, [“Yugoslavia: The Case of Self-Managing Market Socialism”](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.5.4.187).
- U.S. Office of the Historian, [“Eastern Europe, the Soviet Union and Foreign Policy Autonomy,” §§17–21](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v20/d30).
- Council of Europe Observatory on History Teaching, [guidance on multiperspectivity](https://www.coe.int/en/web/observatory-history-teaching/-/integrating-multiperspectivity-in-the-history-classroom).
