[00021] [Heading 1] Babushka Continuity and Yakutsk Setting
[00022] [List Bullet] Working date: circa 2300–2330, roughly two centuries after Hall of Reflections. Yakutsk grew from the Federation/Sakha compromise seen in A Map of Forgotten Stars. Gred and Babushka are deep history, not active institutions.
[00023] [List Bullet] At the end of Hall, Yakutsk is already a surviving command center pushing east, restoring convoys and aircraft, and treating opaque rogue machine systems as existential threats. That memory becomes the constitutional reason later Yakutsk refuses to let JANUS become an invisible sovereign.
[00024] [List Bullet] Sayan Ammosov’s birth journals and attempt to rebuild broken family trees become a cultural ancestor of Continuity: Yakutsk learned during the Signal Famine that a civilization can lose people twice, first physically and then from the record.
[00025] [List Bullet] Kovalenko’s arrival leaves Yakutsk with advanced biomedical knowledge and the unresolved immortality wafer. Over two centuries this becomes one strand of a strong local reproductive and longevity science tradition, eventually feeding ectogenesis and Continuity rather than producing a simple immortal ruling class.
[00026] [List Bullet] Lev-era microtubule research survives as precursor science. By Tomas’s era it has matured into the Closure Problem: synthetic systems can emulate the relevant physics, but doing so at useful scale costs more energy and hardware than growing biological brains. Human minds therefore remain economically necessary.
[00027] [List Bullet] Vorithra remains disputed cosmological history. Most people never experienced it. Historians argue whether late-Gred accounts describe an alien intelligence, psychological filtering, machine contamination, or several phenomena collapsed into one legend. Do not make Vorithra an active force in this novella.
[00028] [Heading 1] Yakutsk in Tomas’s Era
[00029] [List Bullet] Yakutsk is again a major Eurasian city on the Lena. Earth has substantially rebuilt into federations, city-regions, orbital jurisdictions, and trade compacts. Planetary communications, scientific exchange, markets, and diplomacy function normally; isolation is history rather than the present condition.
[00030] [List Bullet] The reconstructed Russian Federation exists, but Yakutsk retains deep Sakha autonomy inherited from the post-collapse compact. Sakha and Russian naming, language, ritual, and institutions coexist. The city should feel locally specific rather than generically Russian.
[00031] [List Bullet] Yakutsk knows the rest of the world well. It receives planetary news, runs interregional research, trades through rail/air/river/orbital systems, and participates in shared JANUS protocols. Critical systems still maintain local fallback modes because the Signal Famine and rogue-AI wars made network dependence culturally suspect.
[00032] [List Bullet] Cold is an economic asset. Dense compute campuses use winter air and Lena-loop liquid cooling. Waste heat from JANUS and Orchestration Houses feeds district heating, greenhouses, gestation wards, baths, and transit galleries. A compute outage can literally make nearby apartments colder.
[00033] [List Bullet] Cold is also a constant engineering enemy. Deep piles, active ground cooling, thermal breaks, monitored soil loops, ice fog, frozen seals, brittle materials, and seasonal river movement should recur naturally in scenes.
[00034] [List Bullet] Do not portray permanent winter. Yakutsk still has violent seasonal contrast: spring breakup on the Lena, summer heat, mosquitoes, smoke, thaw, flooding, and foundation management. The old thaw quarter survives as a historical district above heavily stabilized ground.
[00035] [List Bullet] JANUS is a native post-Babushka AI lineage, not Babushka reborn. It is designed around auditability, human veto, adversarial forks, and contestability precisely because Yakutsk’s ancestors feared opaque machine sovereignty.

[00070] [Heading 1] POV AND NARRATIVE DISTANCE
[00071] [Normal] LOCKED DECISION
[00072] [Normal] Use close third-person limited through Tomas for the entire present-day narrative. Eva never becomes a conventional POV character. Her journals, annotations, recordings, research notes, and final testimony may use first person inside archival material.
[00073] [Normal] WHY CLOSE THIRD
[00074] [Normal] The novella carries a large amount of technical and social argument: JANUS, orchestration, the Closure Problem, tail distributions, preferential attachment, Daughter Continuity, Shadow Futures, and reproductive asymmetry. Close third lets the narrative voice carry some explanatory weight without forcing Tomas to sound as though he is constantly explaining his own civilization to himself.
[00075] [Normal] The distance should remain extremely tight. The reader can share Tomas’s assumptions, anger, mistakes, and private language almost as intimately as first person, while the prose retains a small amount of skepticism toward his interpretations. This matters because Tomas repeatedly mistakes an incomplete historical record for a complete causal story.
[00076] [Normal] WHY NOT FIRST PERSON
[00077] [Normal] First person would intensify Tomas’s obsession with Eva, but it would also make the book feel more like his philosophical memoir. More importantly, it would place Tomas’s voice permanently between the reader and Eva. Close third allows Tomas to recede during long archival sequences so Eva’s recordings and simulations can temporarily dominate the page.
[00078] [Normal] EVA’S FIRST-PERSON MATERIAL
[00079] [Normal] Use first-person Eva material selectively and with force. Because the only biological woman in the novella never receives ordinary POV chapters, the sudden appearance of her own 'I' should feel unusually direct. A line such as 'I have spent six years trying to prove them wrong' can interrupt Tomas’s reconstruction and remind the reader that Eva is not evidence, symbol, martyr, or model. She is a person speaking for herself.
[00080] [Normal] PRACTICAL STYLE RULE
[00081] [Normal] Write Tomas in very close third, freely using fragments, immediate judgments, and his private vocabulary. Avoid distant omniscient commentary. During simulations and archival playback, let the prose move close to Eva’s material for stretches, then re-anchor in Tomas whenever his reaction changes the meaning of what he is seeing.
